Before you record (about 15 minutes ahead)

  1. The lab notebook container is stopped. (Check with ssh ibmz "podman ps": it shouldn't list notebook.)
  2. On the VM: bash ~/safepay/run_safepay.sh starts Granite and SafePay. If both already answer, you can skip this.
  3. On the laptop, open the tunnel and leave that window open: ssh -N -L 8090:127.0.0.1:8090 ibmz
  4. In one Edge window, open two tabs: tab 1 the deck (pitch/SafePay_pitch_deck.pdf; press Ctrl + \ for one page at a time), tab 2 the demo (http://localhost:8090). Switch between them with Ctrl + 1 and Ctrl + 2. Maximise the window, zoom 100%.
  5. On the demo, press Reset just before your first take. Then record the scenes in order: scene 4 creates case #1 and scene 6 creates case #2.
  6. Click each scenario only once. Clicking one again adds more payments to the same case, and the evidence pack would no longer have "all eight".
  7. After scene 6, open http://localhost:8090/analyst and wait until case #2 shows a Granite brief (one to two minutes) before you record scene 8. Takes are separate, so the wait doesn't appear in the video.
  8. On Windows: plug in the charger, close other apps, press Win + N → Do not disturb.

Rehearse once without recording, then press Reset again before the real takes.

The script

Small grey lines are what to show. Large lines are what to say. Record one scene per take. Scenes marked DEMO are in tab 2; the rest are slides in tab 1.

1 · The problem0:00–0:15 · 15 s

Slide 1 (title), then slide 2.

Hi, I'm Suhas, and this is SafePay. When someone escapes an abuser, they block them everywhere. But you can't block a bank transfer, so abusers send one-cent payments with threats in the reference.

2 · Why today's defences fall short0:15–0:30 · 15 s

Slide 3.

Banks use banned-word lists, which abusers dodge with disguised spellings, or by splitting one threat across nine transfers. The best published model scores relationships once a month, so victims are exposed for weeks.

3 · What SafePay does0:30–0:45 · 15 s

Slide 4. On the last sentence, switch to the demo (Ctrl + 2).

SafePay sits inside the payment flow on IBM Z. It reads real ISO 20022 payment messages and makes a decision on every payment in about a millisecond. Here it is, running live.

4 · DEMO · Threat split across nine payments0:45–1:05 · 20 s

Click Threat split across 9 payments. Point at the Joined with recent box in the middle, then Sam's app on the right.

This threat is split across nine transfers, so no single message trips a filter. SafePay joins them up, reads "I am going to find you and kill you", and hides all nine. The money still arrives, because it could be child support.

5 · Three layers1:05–1:17 · 12 s

Slide 5 (Ctrl + 1). On the last word, back to the demo.

That's one of three layers: a message model that undoes disguised spellings, a joiner for split messages, and a relationship model on IBM Snap ML.

6 · DEMO · Polite but menacing1:17–1:35 · 18 s

Click Polite but menacing (coercive control). Point at the message score (about 0.0), then the relationship score.

Here there are no rude words: "saw you at the gym today", "I'm outside". The message scores near zero. But the relationship model sees penny payments at night, never answered, and opens a case.

7 · DEMO · The victim's side1:35–1:55 · 20 s

In Sam's app, open Message settings. Choose In the app only, tick Someone else may see my phone, press Save. Under Riley, choose Application for a protection order → Allow a record. Then press Quick exit.

The victim stays in control. Sam chooses in-app contact only, because someone else may see her phone, and allows a record of Riley's payments for a protection order. One tap on quick exit hides everything.

8 · DEMO · Analyst console and evidence pack1:55–2:17 · 22 s

Before this take, wait until case #2 shows its Granite brief. Click Analyst console, open case #2, point at the safe-contact panel, then the Granite brief. Click Prepare signed evidence pack, then Verify, then Simulate tampering.

The specialist team sees her rules first, and IBM Granite, running on the same machine, drafts their brief. With her consent, they prepare a signed evidence pack of all eight payments. Change one full stop, and verification fails.

9 · Results2:17–2:32 · 15 s

Slide 7 (Ctrl + 1).

Against the bank's monthly approach, SafePay opens a case in a median of 3 days instead of 19, and withholds 92% of abusive messages as they arrive.

10 · Built on IBM Z2:32–2:45 · 13 s

Slide 11.

It all runs on an IBM z17 in LinuxONE Community Cloud: over 900 payments a second, encrypted and pseudonymised, with no data leaving the platform.

11 · Close2:45–2:52 · 7 s

Slide 13.

SafePay: the money gets through, the abuse doesn't, and the evidence does. Thank you.

Recording with Clipchamp (built into Windows 11)

  1. Start → type Clipchamp → Create a new video → Record & create → Screen and camera (or just Screen if you'd rather not appear). Allow the camera and microphone, and pick the Edge window that has the deck and demo tabs, so switching tabs mid-take is recorded too.
  2. One scene per take. Pause for a second before and after speaking; it makes trimming easy. A mistake then costs 20 seconds, not 3 minutes.
  3. Drag the takes onto the timeline in order and trim the gaps. If you used the camera, shrink it to a small circle in the empty area under the demo's middle panel, so it never covers Sam's app or a slide's numbers.
  4. Check the length on the timeline: 3:00 or less.
  5. Export → 1080p. Watch the whole file once.
  6. Upload to YouTube as Unlisted (or Google Drive shared with Anyone with the link) and paste the link into Hack2Skill.
